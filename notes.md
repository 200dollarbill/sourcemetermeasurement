### Measurement Methodology: How and Why Data Was Taken

To accurately determine the field generated by the onboard traces, we had to perform two completely distinct sets of measurements. This two-step methodology is necessary because TMR sensors possess inherent manufacturing variances; we cannot assume a nominal sensitivity for them.

**1. Response Measurements (Library-Based Calibration)**
*   **How it was taken:** The boards were placed inside a testing apparatus (such as a Helmholtz coil) that applies a highly controlled, known, external magnetic field. We swept the external magnetic field (Gauss) and measured the resistance change (Ohms) of each individual TMR sensor.
*   **Why it was taken:** This establishes the "ground truth" performance of every individual sensor on the board. This step isolates the sensor's inherent properties, giving us the **True Sensitivity (Ohms/G)**, Magnetoresistance (MR) Ratio, and hysteresis characteristics. Without this baseline, any onboard trace testing would be completely arbitrary.

**2. Onboard Measurements (Trace Testing)**
*   **How it was taken:** The external magnetic field apparatus was turned off. Instead, a power supply (Kepco) swept a current (Amperes) directly through the printed traces/coils located on the PCB itself. We measured the resulting resistance change (Ohms) in the TMR sensors caused by the localized magnetic field generated by the traces.
*   **Why it was taken:** This tests the actual performance of the PCB trace design. It tells us how effectively the trace converts electrical current into a magnetic field at the physical location of the sensor.

### Data Processing: The "Implied Field"

The data you will be analyzing is the **Implied Field Correlation**. To link the two measurements together, we processed the data using the following logic:
1.  We took the raw Resistance Change (Ohms) from the **Onboard Measurements**.
2.  We divided it by the True Sensitivity (Ohms/G) obtained from the **Response Measurements** for that exact same sensor.
3.  This yielded the **Implied Magnetic Field (Gauss)** actually generated by the onboard trace.
4.  Finally, we ran a linear fit comparing the applied trace current against the Implied Field to calculate the trace efficiency: **Generated Field Correlation (G/A)**.
